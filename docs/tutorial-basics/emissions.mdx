# Emissions

<span className="badge badge--info">Advanced</span>

*Assumes you know Ergo's UTXO model, block rewards and mining pools. For the contract layout behind
emission, read [LFSM](./lfsm.mdx).*

Lithos emits LIT when a queued collateral position becomes active. The schedule advances by
**activations**, not by wall-clock time and not directly by Ergo block height. Each activation removes
the scheduled LIT from the emission box and places that allocation into the new collateral box.

This page covers the LIT supply, the activation-based schedule, the split between public and private
emission, the finder's LIT share, and what happens after emission ends. It only introduces Join,
Activate and Clear where they affect emission. The queue rules themselves are covered in
[LFSM](./lfsm.mdx).

:::info Genesis identifiers are not final
The current mainnet token IDs, contract addresses and genesis constants are placeholders until the
mainnet mint. The supply and emission quantities on this page are protocol parameters, not deployment
identifiers.
:::

## Emission allocations

LIT has a fixed supply of **1,000,000,000 LIT** with 9 decimal places. The supply is divided into one
genesis allocation and four allocations that leave the emission box over time:

| Allocation | Share | LIT | Release path |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Public mining allocation | 68.5% | 685,000,000 | Emitted across collateral activations |
| Community | 17.5% | 175,000,000 | Minted separately at genesis to the community multisig |
| Team | 10% | 100,000,000 | Emitted during the first 1,000,000 activations |
| Investors | 2.5% | 25,000,000 | Emitted during the first 1,000,000 activations |
| Auditors | 1.5% | 15,000,000 | Emitted during the first 1,000,000 activations |

The emission box starts with **825,000,000 LIT**, or `825000000000000000` base units. That balance is
the 685,000,000 LIT public mining allocation plus the 140,000,000 LIT team, investor and advisor
allocations. The 175,000,000 LIT community allocation never sits in the emission box.

The public and private allocations leave the emission box at activation time, but they do not all reach
their final recipients at that moment. The new collateral box carries the exact split until a finder
spends that collateral box in a Lithos block.

### By activation

An **Activate** transaction turns one queue box into one live collateral box. That transition is the
event that advances the LIT schedule.

At a high level, one activation does the following:

1. Spends the current emission box and the queue box selected by the queue policy.
2. Creates the successor emission box with one fewer scheduled LIT allocation.
3. Creates a collateral box carrying the public LIT amount and, while private emission is active, the
   team, investor and advisor amounts.
4. Records the public and private split in collateral `R6`.
5. Increments the emission box's activation counter.

The collateral box then waits in the active set until a finder uses it. A later **genesis transaction**
spends that collateral box to make a block a Lithos block.

The collateral spend releases the LIT that activation reserved. The private allocations go to their
fixed recipients during the first 1,000,000 activations. The public allocation is split between the
finder and the holding box. The holding box starts the rollup that later pays miners according to their
proven work.

## Public emission schedule

One emission epoch is **125,000 activations**. The public rate starts at 500 LIT per activation and
falls over the life of the schedule.

The public rate and its immediate split are:

| Activation epochs | Public LIT per activation | Finder share | LIT entering the holding box | Private LIT per activation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 through 1 | 500 | 20 | 480 | 140 |
| 2 through 3 | 450 | 18 | 432 | 140 |
| 4 through 5 | 400 | 16 | 384 | 140 |
| 6 through 7 | 350 | 14 | 336 | 140 |
| 8 through 9 | 300 | 12 | 288 | 0 |
| 10 through 11 | 250 | 10 | 240 | 0 |
| 12 through 13 | 200 | 8 | 192 | 0 |
| 14 | 150 | 6 | 144 | 0 |
| 15 | 100 | 4 | 96 | 0 |
| 16 onward, until the emission boundary | 50 | 2 | 48 | 0 |

Epoch 22 is truncated. Only its first **75,000 activations** emit LIT. The schedule reaches its
rate-zero phase when the activation counter reaches **2,825,000**.

The public schedule emits exactly **685,000,000 LIT**. The private schedule emits another
**140,000,000 LIT**, so the emission box releases its full 825,000,000 LIT balance over the emitting
region.

## Founder emission schedule

Private/founder emission runs only for the first **8 epochs**, which is the first **1,000,000 activations**.
Its rate is constant at 140 LIT per activation.

Each private allocation uses the following split:

| Recipient group | LIT per activation | Total after 1,000,000 activations |
|---|---:|---:|
| Team | 100 | 100,000,000 |
| Investors | 25 | 25,000,000 |
| Advisors | 15 | 15,000,000 |

These amounts are recorded when the collateral box activates and paid when that collateral box is
spent. The collateral contract requires the private allocation to be paid in full. A transaction
cannot pay only part of the scheduled private split.

After the first 1,000,000 activations, collateral boxes carry no private allocation. Public emission
continues until the final emission boundary.

## Understand the block finder's LIT share

The finder of a Lithos block receives **4% of the public LIT rate** for the collateral box they spend:

```text
finder LIT = public LIT rate / 25
```

The remaining 96% enters the holding box and becomes part of the rollup payout for miners. The finder
share is part of the 685,000,000 LIT public mining allocation. It is not an extra emission on top of
the schedule.

The collateral contract implements this share as transaction slack rather than as a recipient-specific
reward rule. Whatever public LIT the holding box is not required to take remains available to the
finder's transaction. Because the share is proportional, it falls automatically as the public rate
falls and reaches zero when emission reaches its rate-zero phase.

The rollup distributes the holding box's LIT according to miner scores. For how miners prove those
scores, read [NISPs](./NISPs.mdx). Block finders can also earn additional ERG from additional collateral fees,
along with MEV and batching.

## Understanding why emission is usage-indexed

The emission schedule is indexed by activations, not by every Ergo block. This ties most LIT emission
to actual Lithos use.

The active set holds at most **100 collateral boxes**. During bootstrap, Lithos can fill those slots
without first retiring old collateral boxes. Those initial activations are the only part of the
schedule that can advance without prior Lithos block production.

Once the active set is full, a new activation needs a proof-of-spend box from a collateral box that was
already consumed. A finder creates that proof-of-spend when they use collateral in a Lithos block.
After bootstrap, continued activation therefore depends on collateral turnover.

If Lithos produces no blocks, the full active set does not turn over and the emission counter stops
advancing. The schedule does not continue merely because Ergo height increases. **This usage-based approach
also encourages long term development from the foundation**, as founder rewards are only released when the protocol
is doing its job properly and finding blocks.

## The emission box layout

The emission box is a singleton authenticated by the emission NFT. It carries both the remaining LIT
supply and the minimum state needed to manage activations:

| Register or token | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `R4` | Activation counter used by the emission schedule |
| `R5` | Active lender set |
| `R6` | Queue head counter |
| `R7` | Queue tail counter |
| Token index 0 | Emission NFT |
| Token index 1 | Queue tokens |
| Token index 2 | Collateral tokens |
| Token index 3 | Remaining LIT |

LIT sits last in the token list deliberately. When the emission balance reaches zero, the LIT entry can
disappear without shifting the indexes of the emission NFT, queue tokens or collateral tokens.

The emission box also serves as the source of queue and collateral proposition tokens. Those tokens
authenticate queue and collateral positions. Their supply is separate from the LIT schedule.

### Separate policies

The emission box runs two pieces of logic together:

| Component | Controls | Upgrade boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `LIT_Emissions.ergo` | LIT schedule, activation accounting and the three operation paths | Pinned by `Emission_Guard`; fixed at genesis |
| `Collateral_Enforcer.ergo` | Queue selection, permit pricing and Join-side queue policy | Named by the emission config; replaceable |

This split means changing queue policy does not change the LIT supply schedule. A replacement enforcer
can change which valid queue box becomes eligible or how much LIT a permit requires, but the immutable
emission logic still decides how much LIT an activation can release.

The emission logic recognizes exactly three operations:

| Operation | Effect on emission |
|---|---|
| Join | Does not advance the emission counter or release scheduled LIT |
| Activate | Advances the counter and moves the scheduled LIT into a new collateral box |
| Clear | Does not advance the emission counter or release scheduled LIT |

A fourth operation cannot be added after genesis.

## Know what protects the supply

Several rules keep the contract and client on the same schedule.

First, the emission arithmetic is pinned inside `Emission_Guard`. A config update can replace queue
policy, but it cannot replace the schedule itself.

Second, the Lithos client mirrors the contract arithmetic in `EmissionSchedule`. Any client building an
Activate transaction has to calculate the same public and private amounts as `LIT_Emissions.ergo`.
If the values differ, the contract rejects the transaction.

Third, an activation cannot take more LIT than the emission box actually holds. The final emitting
activation is capped by the remaining balance rather than trusting a nominal rate that would overdraw
the box.

Finally, private allocations are all-or-nothing. During the private-emission region, a collateral spend
must satisfy the complete recorded split.

## The end of LIT emissions

Emission ending does **not** stop Lithos.

At the boundary, the public and private rates become zero. Join, Activate and Clear remain valid
operations. The collateral queue can continue rotating positions, and lenders can continue using LIT
permits that already exist in circulation.

This rate-zero phase is explicit in the protocol. The queue does not depend on a positive emission
amount, and the LIT token ID remains the same even after the emission box no longer carries a LIT
entry.

The result is a fixed supply: after the emission box releases its 825,000,000 LIT, no later activation
creates additional LIT.
