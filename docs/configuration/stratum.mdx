# Stratum and difficulty

<span className="badge badge--success">Standard</span>

*For anyone running the Lithos client. No Ergo or smart contract experience needed.*

The `stratum` block controls the local server your mining software connects to, and the difficulty you
commit to on chain. This page covers everything under `stratum` except `stratum.candidate`, which is
large enough to have [its own page](./candidate.mdx).

`stratum.diff` is the most consequential setting in the whole file. It decides both what you are paid
and whether you are paid at all.

## Difficulty

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stratum.diff` | `4.0G` | A parseable difficulty above zero. **Required** | The difficulty you commit to on chain. Your payout share is proportional to it |
| `stratum.diffRefreshInterval` | 60000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms. **Required** | How often the client re-reads your committed difficulty from chain state |
| `stratum.forceConfigDiff` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Mine at `diff` instead of your on-chain commitment. For testing only |

The format is a number followed by a power-of-ten suffix: `K`, `M`, `G`, `T` or `P`. So `4.0G`,
`1.52M` and `300.1K` are all valid. Startup parses the value and refuses anything it cannot read or
that resolves to zero or less.

**Choosing the number is a real decision**, covered in the
[startup guide](../startup-intro.mdx#choosing-a-difficulty). In short: your payout is proportional to
your committed difficulty, but you earn nothing for a block unless you produced 10 super shares in the
60 blocks before it. Set it as high as you can while still reliably clearing 10 - 15 super shares.

### Testing without committing

`forceConfigDiff` makes the client mine at the `diff` in your file rather than at whatever it has
committed on chain. Paired with `state.autoCommit = false`, it lets you measure super share rates at
several difficulties without writing any of them to the blockchain:

```hocon
stratum.forceConfigDiff = true
state.autoCommit = false
```

:::warning Return both to their defaults before mining for real
With `forceConfigDiff = true`, mining below your on-chain commitment produces NISPs that will not be
accepted. The setting exists to measure hardware, not to run on.
:::

## The stratum server

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stratum.stratumPort` | 4444 | 1 to 65535. **Required** | The TCP port your mining software connects to |
| `stratum.connectionTimeout` | 60000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms. **Required** | How long a silent connection is held before it is dropped |
| `stratum.blockRefreshInterval` | 1000 | 100 to 600000 ms. **Required** | How often the server checks whether it has new work to push |
| `stratum.reduceShareMessages` | `true` | `true` or `false`. **Required** | Raise the difficulty reported to your mining software without changing what Lithos pays you at |

### Share traffic

At low difficulties your mining software may send enough shares to trigger errors about duplicate or
old shares. `reduceShareMessages` raises the stratum difficulty by a factor of 10,000, so your mining
software sends only super shares. **The difficulty Lithos evaluates and pays you at is unchanged.**
Besides the reduced traffic, another benefit is that you can directly see the number of super shares you made
in your mining software. With this setting on, all accepted shares are automatically super shares, so it can
easily be read in the mining softwares CLI output.

It ships on, and there generally no reason to turn it off outside debugging.

## Extra nonce

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stratum.extraNonce1Size` | 2 | 1 to 8 bytes. **Required** | Hex bytes of `extraNonce1` handed to each connection |
| `stratum.rotateExtraNonceInterval` | 0 | 0 to 3600000 ms | How long a connection may go without submitting work before its `extraNonce1` is rotated. `0` disables rotation |

`extraNonce1` splits the nonce space between connections so two rigs never search the same range. The
size trades that split against the space each rig gets to work through in a single job.

**Two is recommended and shipped.** It leaves each rig a large nonce space for one job. Raising it
narrows that space, which invites duplicate shares and extra nonce changes in your mining software.

Rotation exists only for setups that raised the size above 2 and are running out of distinct values.
At the shipped size you should not need it, which is why it ships disabled.
