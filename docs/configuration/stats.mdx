# Statistics and web panels

<span className="badge badge--info">Advanced</span>

*Assumes you know your client reads everything from your Ergo node, and what a node read costs. Start with [Node connection and security](./node.mdx) if not.*

The `stats` block controls the statistics behind the client's web panels: your stratum's hashrate and
shares, your Lithos blocks and payments, the collateral market, and the LithosDex graphs. This page
covers what each part collects, how often it reads your node, and how much history it keeps on disk.

**Statistics never affect mining.** The collector runs on its own threads and nothing on the mining
path waits for it. Turning any of it off blanks the matching panels and changes nothing else.

## Turning statistics on and off

The following switches decide what is collected:

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Collect statistics at all. Off, every panel reports `disabled` |
| `stats.mining.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Collect Lithos block, payment, difficulty and collateral history for the Mining and Collateral panels |
| `stats.dex.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Collect LithosDex pool history for the LithosDex graphs |
| `stats.storage.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Keep history on disk across restarts. Off, history lives in memory and starts over at every restart |

## Your stratum

These settings cover what your own mining software reports to the stratum server:

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats.refreshIntervalMs` | 1000 | 100 to 60000 ms | How often the stratum's hashrate and share counts are published |
| `stats.staleAfterMs` | 15000 | More than `refreshIntervalMs`, at most 3600000 ms | How old a published reading may be before the panel marks it stale |

These readings come from the stratum itself, so they cost no node reads.

## Mining and collateral history

The Mining and Collateral panels read your Lithos blocks, payments and difficulty epochs from the chain.
The client walks the chain a few blocks at a time and keeps what it found:

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats.mining.historyDays` | 30 | 1 to 36500 days | How far back the first walk reaches, and how long history is kept after it |
| `stats.mining.pruning.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Remove history older than `historyDays` |
| `stats.mining.refreshIntervalMs` | 10000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms | Gap between refreshes |
| `stats.mining.staleAfterMs` | 120000 | More than `refreshIntervalMs`, at most 86400000 ms | How old the history may be before the panel marks it stale |
| `stats.mining.blocksPerRefresh` | 20 | 1 to 100 | Blocks one refresh reads |
| `stats.mining.readTimeoutMs` | 3000 | 100 to 30000 ms | Deadline on one node read |
| `stats.mining.readBudgetMs` | 20000 | 1000 to 120000 ms | Deadline on one whole refresh |

**Totals on the Mining panel cover retained history only.** A payment older than `historyDays` is
pruned and no longer counted. Raise `historyDays` before the first start if you want a longer record,
because the first walk only reaches that far back.

On a fresh start the client walks `historyDays` of chain at `blocksPerRefresh` blocks per refresh, so
the panels fill in over the first minutes rather than at once.

## LithosDex history

The LithosDex graphs read the pool's past boxes from your node's index:

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats.dex.refreshIntervalMs` | 30000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms | Delay after one refresh finishes before the next starts |
| `stats.dex.staleAfterMs` | 120000 | More than `refreshIntervalMs`, at most 86400000 ms | How old the graphs may be before the panel marks them stale |
| `stats.dex.readTimeoutMs` | 3000 | 100 to 30000 ms | Deadline on one node read |
| `stats.dex.refreshBudgetMs` | 20000 | 100 to 120000 ms | Deadline on one whole refresh |
| `stats.dex.historyPages` | 10 | 1 to 50 | Pages of 200 pool boxes read per refresh, newest first |
| `stats.dex.timestampLookups` | 128 | 0 to 250 | Block headers read per refresh to put times on the pool's history |

The graphs reach back at most 30 days, plus the window before that for comparison. `historyPages`
caps how many pool boxes a refresh reads to get there, so a very busy pool can show a shorter span.
Only one of these reads runs at a time, which is why the interval is measured from the end of a
refresh rather than its start.

## Storing history on disk

History is kept in a LevelDB store under your client folder:

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats.storage.backend` | `leveldb` | A backend the client can resolve | Which key-value store to use |
| `stats.storage.path` | `.lithos/stats` | A non-empty path | Where that store lives |
| `stats.storage.flushIntervalMs` | 5000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms | How often LithosDex observations are written |
| `stats.storage.sampleIntervalMinutes` | 5 | 1 to 1440 minutes | One stored LithosDex observation per interval. Readings between them are merged |
| `stats.storage.pruning.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Remove stored LithosDex observations older than `retentionDays` |
| `stats.storage.pruning.retentionDays` | 30 | 1 to 36500 days | How long stored LithosDex observations are kept |
| `stats.storage.pruning.intervalMs` | 60000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms | Gap between pruning passes |
| `stats.storage.pruning.batchSize` | 256 | 1 to 1440 | Observations removed per pruning pass |

Mining history is stored under the same path, in its own database, and pruned by
`stats.mining.historyDays`. Deleting `.lithos/stats` while the client is stopped is safe: the client
rebuilds the history from the chain on its next start.

:::note A client upgrade can start the mining history over
The mining history database is versioned. When an upgrade changes its layout, the client leaves the old
database untouched and rebuilds a new one from the chain, which takes the first walk described above.
:::
