# Collateral and emission

<span className="badge badge--info">Advanced</span>

*Assumes you have read how collateral and the permit work in [Lithos Protocol](../intro.mdx).*

The `emission` block covers two different jobs that share a timer. The first is **driving the queue**,
which every client does and which costs only transaction fees. The second is **joining the queue with
your own funds**, which locks ERG and LIT until a miner spends the box, and which ships off.

This is maintenance that runs whether or not you ever mine a block.

:::danger A queued position cannot be withdrawn
Once your ERG and permit are in the queue, neither returns until a block spends the box. There is no
cancel path, by design. Everything under [Lending your own funds](#lending-your-own-funds) is a
commitment rather than a setting you can back out of.
:::

## Driving the queue

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `emission.enabled` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Drive the collateral queue at all |
| `emission.queueInterval` | 240000 | 1000 to 3600000 ms | How often to look at the head of the queue |
| `emission.maxQueueSpends` | 4 | 1 to 128 | Emission spends chained in one pass, which is how deep into the queue a pass reaches |
| `emission.queueScanLimit` | 500 | 1 to 100000 | Queue boxes paged through when looking for the head |
| `emission.txFee` | 1000000 | The network minimum fee to 1000000000 nanoERG | Fee on emission transactions |

Activating the head of the queue turns a waiting position into a live collateral box, which is what
keeps collateral boxes available for everyone to mine against. Clearing removes a box whose lender key
is already active and which would otherwise hold the whole queue still.

**Both are permissionless, and clients are encouraged to keep this on so that pool liveness is mainained.** 

`txFee` is floored at the network minimum because a transaction below it will not relay.

## Chaining off the mempool

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `emission.mempoolChaining` | `true` | `true` or `false` | Chain off unconfirmed spends of the emission box rather than waiting for confirmation |
| `emission.maxChainDepth` | 16 | 0 to 4096 | Unconfirmed spends followed before falling back to confirmed state |

During a join burst the confirmed emission box is several spends behind the real tip, so without
chaining the queue can barely be driven at all.

**The unconfirmed spends being chained onto belong to other lenders.** They are chained onto to ensure maximum
effectiveness and queue slot availability.

## Lending your own funds

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `emission.autoCollateralize` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Put your own ERG and LIT into the queue |
| `emission.collateralizeInterval` | 900000 | 1000 to 86400000 ms | How often to check whether more collateral is wanted |
| `emission.maxJoinsPerRun` | 5 | 1 to 128 | Queue boxes created in one pass |
| `emission.maxOwnCollateral` | 10 | 0 to 2000 | Your boxes live at once, across the queue, live collateral and the active set |
| `emission.maxLenderKeys` | 32 | 1 to 1000, and at most `node.numAddresses` | Wallet addresses dedicated to lending |
| `emission.maxPermitPerJoin` | 40000000000000 | Any positive value, in LIT base units | The most LIT to post as a permit on one box |
| `emission.priorityFeeNanoErgs` | 0 | `0`, or 1000000 to 3000000000 nanoERG | ERG each position adds above the 2.915 ERG floor, to be spent sooner |

### What a position costs

Each live box locks **2.915 ERG plus its permit** until a miner spends it. So `maxOwnCollateral` is
your total exposure ceiling, not a throughput setting: at the default of 10 you can have about 29 ERG
plus ten permits committed at once.

`maxPermitPerJoin` is a **price you are willing to pay, not a fee**. The permit rises with the queue
backlog, from a floor of 2,000 LIT toward a ceiling of 40,000. When the thermostat passes your cap, the
run stops rather than paying it. The default is the protocol ceiling itself, so out of the box price
never blocks a join.

The value is in base units at 9 decimals, so 40,000 LIT is `40000000000000`.

### Paying for priority

A miner who finds a block spends the collateral box that pays them most, so a position that offers
more is spent ahead of the ones that offer nothing. `priorityFeeNanoErgs` is that offer, for the
positions `autoCollateralize` creates. A join from the web panel sets its own. Each position then locks `2.915 ERG + priorityFeeNanoErgs`, and when a miner spends it the fee divides as follows:

| Share | Goes to |
|---|---|
| One fifth | The miner who spends the box |
| Four fifths | The rollup that block opens, paid to every miner who submits to it |

Because transaction fees in a Lithos block go to the lender, paying for priority works like a fee
future: you pay to be spent sooner, for example when you expect the blocks ahead to carry high fees.

`0` posts at the floor and offers nothing. Any other value must be at least `1000000` (0.001 ERG), so
the miner's fifth is worth more than the output it arrives in.

:::warning Past 0.085 ERG the block reward no longer covers your principal
The lender receives the 3 ERG block reward plus the block's transaction fees. Above `85000000`
(0.085 ERG) the priority fee takes the principal past 3 ERG, so getting it all back depends on the
fees in the block that spends your box. The client allows it, and logs a warning on every
self-collateralization pass that posts above this.
:::

### One key per box, and why it matters

The active set holds distinct **keys**. One live box needs one address, and a key already in the set
produces a box nobody can activate.

:::danger A reused lender key forfeits the whole box
A queue box whose key is already active can only be cleared, and clearing forfeits both the principal
and the permit to whoever builds the clearing transaction. This is why `maxLenderKeys` may not exceed
`node.numAddresses`, and why the client refuses a manual join while `autoCollateralize` is on: the
automated pass owns key selection and a manual join could take a key it is already using.
:::

Startup checks the pair and names the value to raise:

```text
emission.maxLenderKeys
    64 exceeds node.numAddresses (32). Addresses past index 31 would receive funds this
    client cannot sign for. Raise node.numAddresses to at least 64
```
