# Tasks and dispatchers

<span className="badge badge--warning">Expert</span>

*Assumes you know what an Akka dispatcher is and why thread pool isolation matters. Most miners never change anything on this page.*

Three blocks are left. `state` holds two switches: one that commits your difficulty on chain and one
that turns protocol work off. `lithos-tasks` configures the three background timers, and
`lithos-contexts` defines the thread pools everything runs on.

The dispatcher pools in particular encode a design decision rather than a preference, and the page says
which ones you must not merge.

## Protocol switches

| Setting | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state.autoCommit` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Register this miner and commit `stratum.diff` on chain, then keep the commitment equal to it |
| `state.disableTransforms` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Disable every transaction and interaction with LFSM rollups |

`disableTransforms` is a blunt instrument. With it on the client follows the chain but submits nothing:
no NISPs, no phase transforms, no fraud proofs, no payouts, and no commitments. **You mine and are not
paid.** This can be useful for testing.

### Committing your difficulty

A miner is paid from a rollup only after two things are on chain: a registration in the **Miner
Dictionary**, the on-chain registry of Lithos miners, and a **difficulty commitment**, the score your
NISPs are judged against. `autoCommit` makes the client handle both.

**It ships off**, so a new client never writes a difficulty you are still testing. While it is off and
you are not registered, the client mines normally but cannot submit NISPs, and the log says so at
startup. Turn it on once you have chosen `stratum.diff`.

With it on, the dictionary task checks every minute, starting about a minute after startup:

1. If you are not registered, the client registers you with `stratum.diff` as your first commitment.
   Registration waits until the Miner Dictionary has synced.
2. If you are registered and your newest commitment differs from `stratum.diff`, the client replaces
   it as soon as the contracts allow.
3. After sending either transaction, the client waits for it to confirm and for its own sync to reach
   that block, then checks again.
4. Once the confirmed commitment equals `stratum.diff`, the loop stops until the next restart.

The contracts decide how fast a commitment moves. The following counts blocks from the moment the
client sends it:

| Event | Blocks after sending | Testnet | Mainnet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The commitment binds, and NISPs are judged against it | 125 | about 1 hour 35 minutes | about 4 hours 10 minutes |
| The commitment may be replaced by a new one | 845 | about 10 hours 30 minutes | about 28 hours |

A registration or change has 5 blocks to be included. If it misses them it becomes invalid, and the
client sends a new one on a later check.

While the loop is waiting, it logs the reason once, as `Auto-commit waiting on ...`, and again only when
the reason changes. `autoCommit` does nothing while `disableTransforms` is true or the dictionary task is
disabled.

:::tip Choose `diff` before you turn this on
A commitment holds for 845 blocks before it can be replaced. Measure your super share rate first with
`stratum.forceConfigDiff = true`, as in
[Stratum and difficulty](./stratum.mdx#testing-without-committing).
:::

## Background tasks

Three tasks run on timers. Each takes the same three keys, and **all three are required** for each
task:

| Key | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `enabled` | `true` or `false` | Whether the task runs |
| `startup` | 1 to 604800000 ms | Delay before its first run |
| `interval` | 1 to 604800000 ms | Gap between runs |

The three tasks and what they ship as:

| Task | Enabled | Startup | Interval | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lithos-tasks.stratum-server` | `true` | 30 seconds | 30 seconds | Runs the stratum server that your mining software connects to |
| `lithos-tasks.rollup-sync-task` | `true` | 10 seconds | 500 millis | Drives rollup phase work against current state |
| `lithos-tasks.dictionary-sync-task` | `true` | 60 seconds | 60 seconds | Registers and commits when `state.autoCommit` is on, and warns before your registration expires |

Durations take any form the config library parses, such as `500 millis`, `30 seconds` or `5 minutes`.
The dictionary task runs at most once a minute, so a shorter `interval` there is raised to one minute.

**A missing task block stops the client** rather than defaulting, because a task silently not running
is worse than a refusal to start. The one exception is a lookup that only picks a log message, which
reads an absent block as disabled.

:::warning The dictionary task is what warns you about expiry
A registration lasts 919,800 blocks and then stops being honoured. This task reads your own entry and
warns from 720 blocks before expiry onward, naming the height after which removal is safe. Disabling it
removes that warning, and removing a live registration at the wrong moment slashes your own honest
submissions.
:::

## Dispatchers

`lithos-contexts` defines every thread pool the client uses. **Startup checks that each named block
exists** and leaves the contents to Akka, so a missing block is caught and a bad pool size is not.

The client requires all twenty-three of these blocks:

| Dispatcher | Pool | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| `wallet-io-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | Candidate requests, which change the node's cached work |
| `wallet-maintenance-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 8 | Inventory scans and reward sweeps |
| `critical-wallet-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | Wallet work that must not queue behind optional work |
| `critical-tx-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 4 | Transactions that must not be delayed |
| `engine-candidate-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 4 | Engine work for candidate assembly |
| `engine-io-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | General engine node I/O |
| `mempool-io-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | Mempool reads |
| `candidate-io-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | Candidate node I/O |
| `mining-control-io-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 4 | One chain observation and one solved-block submission, concurrently |
| `genesis-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 4 | Collateral genesis signing |
| `batching-io-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 4 | ErgoDEX order discovery, broadcasts and builds |
| `lithosdex-batching-io-dispatcher` | 2 threads, queue 4 | The same for LithosDex |
| `snapshot-io-dispatcher` | 4 threads, queue 4 | Snapshot save, restore and dictionary reconstruction |
| `stratum-dispatcher` | fork-join, 4 to 16 | The stratum server |
| `polling-dispatcher` | 32 threads | Node polling |
| `sync-dispatcher` | fork-join, 2 to 10 | Synchronization |
| `tx-dispatcher` | fork-join, 2 to 10 | General transaction work |
| `dex-dispatcher` | 16 threads | The local LithosDex HTTP endpoints |
| `database-dispatcher` | 2 threads | Local database work |
| `stats-dispatcher` | 1 thread | The statistics collector behind the web panels |
| `stats-read-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 1 | LithosDex statistics reads from the node |
| `stats-store-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 1 | Writing LithosDex statistics to disk |
| `mining-stats-dispatcher` | 1 thread, queue 1 | Mining statistics: node reads and their history on disk |

A top-level `stats-mailbox` block also bounds the statistics collector's mailbox at 256 messages, so
a slow statistics read drops observations rather than growing memory. See
[Statistics and web panels](./stats.mdx).

### Why they are separate

The separation is the point, and three of these exist because merging them cost blocks.

**A found block must never queue behind node I/O.** `mining-control-io-dispatcher` has two threads so
that one chain observation and one solved-block submission can run at once, and neither waits for
candidate HTTP, wallet work or transaction preparation. An early version ran a rollup batch inside the
same mailbox that answers block assembly, and every block in flight silently lost its inserted
transactions with nothing in the log connecting the two.

**Candidate requests are serialised on purpose.** `wallet-io-dispatcher` has exactly one thread because
asking the node for a candidate *replaces* the node's cached work. Two concurrent requests would each
mutate a cache the other depends on. Optional transaction workers cannot occupy that thread.

**Genesis signing has its own worker** so that background collateral refresh and ordinary transaction
work cannot occupy it while cached inputs are ready for a new block.

**Snapshot work blocks in LevelDB**, sometimes for a long time. Four threads cover the one save, one
restore and two dictionary reconstructions the actor may own, and the bounded queue contains work left
behind by an unexpected restart when a backend call cannot be interrupted.

:::danger Do not merge these pools
The thread counts are small enough to look wasteful and are not. Every one-thread pool here is a
serialisation guarantee, and every separate pool is an isolation guarantee for work that must not wait
behind something slower. Raising a count is usually safe; merging two blocks is not.
:::
